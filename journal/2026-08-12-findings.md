# Research Journal — 2026-08-12

All figures below were recomputed directly from the data files in this
repo at the time of writing (not carried over from memory or prior chat
output). Source file for each finding is noted so it can be re-verified.

## Strategy under study

Intraday short strangle on NIFTY 50: sold at market open on expiry day
(same-day expiry, i.e. the contract's `XpryDt` equals the trade date),
closed before that same day's close. Never held overnight. Paper trading
only — no broker, no order execution.

## Finding 1 — Index move distribution

Source: `data/raw/nifty50_daily.csv` (yfinance `^NSEI`, 2016-01-01 to
today), 2,613 trading days.

- Average absolute open-to-close move: **0.569%**
- Median absolute move: **0.415%**
- Std deviation: **0.605%**
- Moves over 1.0%: **15.19%** of days

The average sitting well above the median indicates a fat-tailed
distribution — most days are quiet, but a minority of large-move days
pull the mean up.

## Finding 2 — Breach rate against the hold-to-expiry zone

Using a hold-to-expiry-style profit zone of **+1.11% / −1.27%** from the
open (asymmetric, matching a real strangle's strike placement):

- Combined breach rate: **10.68%** of days
- Upside breaches (move > +1.11%): **4.90%**
- Downside breaches (move < −1.27%): **5.78%**

Downside breaches exceed upside breaches despite the downside boundary
being *wider* — consistent with NIFTY historically having sharper
down-moves than up-moves.

## Finding 3 — Breakevens compress under intraday exit (reference price corrected)

Source: `data/processed/nifty_expiry_day_chains.csv`, 2026-08-11.

The +1.11%/−1.27% zone in Findings 1–2 assumes collecting the full
premium at expiry. That's not this strategy — the position is closed
same-day. Measured against real quoted premiums for the 2026-08-11
expiry-day chain:

| Leg | Strike | OpnPric |
|---|---|---|
| Short Call | 24700 CE | 24.85 |
| Short Put | 24450 PE | 23.85 |

Combined credit: **48.70 points**.

**Correction made during this analysis:** the first pass used
`UndrlygPric` (24,471.70) as the reference spot price for the breakeven
calculation. `UndrlygPric` is NSE's settlement-time underlying reference
in the bhavcopy — it is *not* the market open. The bhavcopy file has no
open-of-index field at all. The correct reference is the actual NIFTY
index open that day, **24,575.10** (yfinance `^NSEI`, confirmed against
`data/raw/nifty50_daily.csv`'s `Open` column and now also merged directly
onto every row of the processed dataset as `NIFTY_Open`).

Recomputed with the correct reference:

- Upside breakeven: 24700 + 48.70 = 24,748.70 → **+0.706%** from open
- Downside breakeven: 24450 − 48.70 = 24,401.30 → **+0.707%** from open (i.e. −0.707%)

So the real same-day profit zone for this specific trade was **≈ ±0.71%**,
not ±1.1%. Against an average daily absolute move of 0.57% (Finding 1),
this zone is only modestly wider than a typical day's move. The 10.68%
breach rate in Finding 2 is measured against a wider, expiry-style zone
and therefore **understates** the true breach rate for an intraday-exit
strangle sized like this one. A proper backtest needs to compute the
breakeven zone per trade from actual strike/credit data, not assume a
fixed ±1.1%/±1.27%.

## Finding 4 — Stop-loss timing is unknowable from EOD data

Source: `data/processed/nifty_expiry_day_chains.csv`, 2026-08-11, 24450 PE.

| OpnPric | HghPric | LwPric | ClsPric |
|---|---|---|---|
| 23.85 | 54.15 | 0.05 | 7.60 |

The put opened at 23.85, reached a high of 54.15 (over 2x the opening
premium — a stop-loss level a real trader would likely use would have
been touched), and separately fell to a low of 0.05, all within the same
session. Daily OHLC data has no timestamp ordering — it cannot tell us
whether the high came before or after the low. This is a structural limit
of end-of-day bhavcopy data, not something more data cleaning fixes.
**Convention adopted for any future backtest:** assume worst-case
ordering (the adverse excursion happens before any favorable move),
since that's the only assumption that doesn't silently flatter results.

## Finding 5 — Clustering

Source: `data/raw/nifty50_daily.csv`, same breach definition as Finding 2.

- Longest run of consecutive breach days: **10 trading days**, 2020-03-09
  to 2020-03-23 (COVID crash)
- Number of 5-trading-day rolling windows containing 2+ breach days: **308**
- Breach days by year: 2016=35, 2017=7, 2018=25, 2019=28, **2020=63**,
  2021=34, 2022=34, **2023=3**, 2024=24, 2025=12, 2026=14 (partial year)

Breach days cluster in time rather than occurring independently. Position
sizing should account for multi-day bad stretches, not just single-day
tail risk.

## Finding 6 — VIX filter is not yet evaluable on P&L

Source: `outputs/filter_analysis.md`, India VIX (yfinance `^INDIAVIX`).

Filtering out days where VIX opened above 15 cuts the breach rate from
10.68% to **4.70%**, but skips **50.81%** of all trading days. Two open
problems before this is usable:

1. High VIX days also carry higher option premiums — the filter may be
   removing the best-paid trading days along with the risky ones. Net
   effect on P&L is unknown until premiums are actually modeled per day.
2. A fixed threshold (VIX > 15) is regime-dependent — "15" meant
   something different in 2016 than it might in 2026 — and effectively
   bakes in hindsight about what counts as "high." A relative threshold
   (e.g. percentile of trailing 12-month VIX) should be tested instead of
   a fixed number.

## Data-quality finding — the Tuesday-expiry assumption doesn't hold for the full sample

Source: `data/raw/download_manifest.csv`, extractor validation output.

The general pipeline (`src/download_bhavcopy.py`) assumed NIFTY's weekly
expiry falls on Tuesday, based on the one sample file we'd already
confirmed (2026-08-11). Downloading every Tuesday over the trailing 12
months and checking for actual same-day-expiry NIFTY option rows
surfaced **4 Tuesdays with zero such rows**: 2025-08-12, 2025-08-19,
2025-08-26, and 2025-10-21. Every Tuesday from 2025-09-02 onward has
expiry-day rows.

This is consistent with NSE's move to standardize weekly index-derivative
expiries to Tuesday, which took effect around September 2025 — before
that, NIFTY's weekly expiry was on a different weekday. 2025-10-21 is a
residual outlier within the Tuesday era, most likely a holiday-shifted
expiry that week; not independently confirmed.

**Practical effect:** the processed dataset (`data/processed/nifty_expiry_day_chains.csv`)
currently contains **46 distinct expiry dates**, from 2025-09-02 to
2026-08-11 — not ~52, since the Tuesday-expiry regime is younger than
the 12-month download window. Extending the sample further back in time
will require detecting the pre-September-2025 expiry weekday separately;
naively adding more Tuesdays before that date will keep returning zero
rows.

  not yet generalized across the 46-date sample)?

## Known limitations

- EOD bhavcopy data only — no intraday price sequence (Finding 4).
- Opening prices on thin/illiquid contracts may be unrepresentative of a
  tradeable fill; no minimum-volume/OI filter has been applied yet.
- Transaction costs and bid-ask spread are currently excluded entirely
  (not yet a configurable switch — this should be added before any P&L
  conclusions are drawn).
- Sample is currently 46 expiry dates (2025-09-02 to 2026-08-11), not a
  full year, due to the expiry-weekday transition described above.
- Finding 3's breakeven compression is based on a single date's option
  chain — needs to be computed across the full 46-date sample before
  drawing a general conclusion about zone width vs. typical daily move.
