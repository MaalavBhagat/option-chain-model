This note sits BESIDE outputs/pledged_leverage_analysis.csv. That CSV has NOT
been edited. It is kept exactly as committed (2a0ff3e) as the historical
record of a run whose P&L booking turned out to be indefensible -- this
project does not silently patch a superseded finding.

The DESIGN of this study is sound and was not withdrawn. Only the price at
which forced-close losses were BOOKED was wrong.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE SPECIFIC FLAW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The forced-close rule is correct and necessary: if peak intraday
mark-to-market loss exceeds cash C, the position is shut rather than carried
quietly to the close.

The error was in what price that forced close was booked at. The run measured
the intraday worst case by taking BOTH short legs at their OWN daily High
price (rows with mtm_measure = "high"), then used that same worst-tick price
as the REALIZED EXIT PRICE.

The call's daily high and the put's daily high need not occur at the same
moment. A short strangle is hurt by the index moving hard in ONE direction,
which pushes one leg up while pushing the other down. Both legs at their
individual daily highs simultaneously describes a moment that, on most days,
never existed.

The study's OWN pre-registration said this in advance -- method (b) is
"a conservative upper bound on a price path that may never have actually
existed, not a measured worst moment. It's a ceiling, not an observation."
The run then treated that ceiling as an observation.

Using a never-observed price to TRIGGER a forced close is conservative and
defensible. Using it to SIZE the realized loss records an impossible loss as
a real one.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS INVALID IN THIS FILE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

INVALID -- every rupee and points TOTAL on rows where mtm_measure = "high":
Arm A and Arm B realized totals, gross premium, return on C, and worst-trade
loss under that measure.

Its most visible symptom: Arm B's high-based totals are NEGATIVE at every
cash level, and in all 36 beta-by-haircut-by-cash cells. Arm A's high-based
totals are POSITIVE at every cash level, in all 36 cells. The flawed booking
hit the levered arm disproportionately because Arm B holds double the lots --
the same impossible per-lot loss applied to twice the position was enough to
drive Arm B negative while leaving Arm A positive. Any statement that "the
high-based totals came out negative" describes ARM B ONLY and must not be
repeated as a blanket claim about both arms.

STILL VALID:
  - Forced-close COUNTS under both measures, and the Arm A vs Arm B
    comparison of those counts. Counting "did peak loss exceed C" does not
    depend on the price the exit is booked at. The corrected run's S3 counts
    reproduce this file's high-based counts exactly, confirming the trigger
    formula never changed.
  - All close-based rows (mtm_measure = "close"). A closing price is a real
    observed number. These are reproduced identically as scenario S2 in the
    corrected file.
