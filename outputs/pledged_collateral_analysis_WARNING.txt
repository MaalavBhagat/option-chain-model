not silently patch a superseded finding.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE SPECIFIC FLAW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The study defined the account's CASH (C) as a FRACTION of that same trade's
own MARGIN REQUIREMENT (the base case used 50%).

Available margin was A = C + min(U, C), where U is the haircut-adjusted value
of the pledged shares. When C is half the margin requirement and the pledged
pot is large enough that min(U, C) returns C, then A = 2C = the margin
requirement exactly. A Type 1 (margin) breach requires the requirement to
EXCEED A. Since A was constructed to EQUAL the requirement, a Type 1 breach
was arithmetically impossible except through rounding.

The result "zero Type 1 breaches across 137 trades" is therefore not a finding
about risk. It is the definition of C, restated.

THE SIGNATURE, VISIBLE IN THE FILE ITSELF:
  - At cash_fraction_pct = 50 (base case), hedged rows: minimum coverage_ratio
    across the whole sample is EXACTLY 1.0000, and type1_margin_breach is
    0 of 2,484 rows. A real stress test produces a distribution; a minimum
    pinned at exactly 1.0000 for the worst of 137 trades is the fingerprint
    of a quantity computed from itself.
  - At cash_fraction_pct = 0 (sensitivity): ALL 2,484 rows breach, coverage
    ratio 0.0. Equally mechanical, equally uninformative.

COMPOUNDING PROBLEM -- THE POT COULD NOT BIND:
  The pledged pot was Rs 10,00,000 against a largest single-trade hedged
  margin requirement of Rs 29,295 -- a ratio of 34.1x. A collateral pot 34
  times larger than anything it must cover cannot be stressed by anything in
  this sample, however C is defined.

CONSEQUENCE FOR BETA:
  Beta could not have affected either breach test under this design. Type 1
  was pinned by construction; Type 2 (cash breach) compared peak MTM loss to
  C alone, so the collateral pot and its beta never entered that comparison
  either. The original prediction 4 ("beta 1.3 produces more breaches than
  beta 0.7") could not have passed under ANY beta value.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS WITHDRAWN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Predictions 1, 2 and 4 of the 2026-08-17 pre-registration, withdrawn as
    evidence -- not miscomputed, but incapable of producing any other answer.
  - The reported 1,638% return-on-posted-cash headline. It is a capital-
    efficiency artifact of dividing by a small C that was itself derived from
    a small margin requirement -- a return on something that was never
    actually stressed.

WHAT STANDS: predictions 3 and 5 of that pre-registration, neither of which
depended on how C was defined relative to the margin requirement.

The replacement design fixes this by making C a FIXED account balance chosen
in advance (Rs 25,000 / 50,000 / 1,00,000 / 2,00,000, each tested
independently), never derived from a trade's own margin requirement, and by
resizing the pot to Rs 2,00,000 -- roughly 7-8x the largest requirement
instead of 34x -- so it can actually bind.

NOTE: the replacement study has its own limitation, disclosed in its journal
entry -- the pot still never bound below C = Rs 2,00,000, so the underlying
collateral-risk (wrong-way-risk) question remains untested.
